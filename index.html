Course Overview

This class is a theoretical and practical study of computer networks: how they're structured, how protocols work together, how data finds its way from one place to another reliably and efficiently. You'll work through the core layers of the internet, from applications down to physical infrastructure, and get hands-on practice with sockets, packet analysis, and network programming.

Alongside the technical material, the course looks at where networks physically operate and who they do or don't serve. New Mexico's broadband landscape, including rural and tribal connectivity, is used throughout the semester to ground abstract concepts in real infrastructure and real communities. Most Tuesdays sessions are discussion-based, working through both classic networking papers and more applied readings; Thursdays are more traditional lectures with in-class exercises.

Learning Objectives

All students:

  • Explain the layered architecture of networks and the role of protocols at each layer
  • Analyze tradeoffs in network design, such as reliability vs. efficiency or wired vs. wireless
  • Connect networking concepts to real, local lived experiences, including questions of access and the digital divide
  • Engage with current issues, emerging research, and ongoing debates in the networking field

Graduate students, additionally:

  • Scope an independent group project at the intersection of networking and a domain of their choosing
  • Teach the class a new concept or skill through a final project presentation

Texts & Readings

Primary Text

Kurose & Ross, Computer Networking: A Top-Down Approach (8th or 9th edition).

For your convenience, all readings are summarized below. Please check the Calendar for the most up-to-date information on required readings and due dates.

Most readings will be available as PDFs or links to the original sources.You are responsible for securing access to the primary textbook.

Foundational Readings

  • Saltzer, Reed & Clark, "End-to-End Arguments in System Design" (1984)
  • Clark, "The Design Philosophy of the DARPA Internet Protocols" (1988)
  • Clark, Wroclawski, Sollins & Braden, "Tussle in Cyberspace: Defining Tomorrow's Internet" (2002)
  • Edwards, Jackson, Bowker & Knobel, "Understanding Infrastructure: Dynamics, Tensions, and Design" (2007)

Applied Readings

  • Palacios Abad et al., "Mending the Fabric: The Contentious, Collaborative Work of Repairing Broadband Maps" (CSCW 2024)
  • Johnson, Jang, O'Rourke, Ye & Heimerl, "Network Capacity as Common Pool Resource" (2021)
  • Hardy, Wyche & Veinot, "Rural HCI Research: Definitions, Distinctions, Methods, and Opportunities" (2019)
  • Woo, Fraire, Ratnasamy, Shenker & Hasan, "Anyone, Anywhere, not Everyone, Everywhere: Starlink Doesn't End the Digital Divide" (HotNets 2025)
